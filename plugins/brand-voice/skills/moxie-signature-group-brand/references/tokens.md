# Moxie Signature Group Visual Tokens

Reference for any visual deliverable: listings, presentations, websites, social media, marketing collateral, signage.

## Brand mark

### Primary icon
- Geometric endless knot
- Use as the primary brand mark across all touchpoints
- Never substitute with keys, house silhouettes, doors, or generic real estate imagery
- The endless knot signals continuity, intention, and craftsmanship

### Logo lockup
- Endless knot icon paired with "Moxie Signature Group" wordmark in Playfair Display
- Stacked or horizontal layouts both acceptable
- Always preserve clear space around the logo (minimum: equal to the height of the knot)

## Typography

### Headlines
- Font: Playfair Display
- Weight: Regular or Semibold (Bold only for very large display use)
- Use for: H1, H2, listing titles, hero text, section headers
- Voice: refined, editorial, intentional

### Body and UI
- Font: Montserrat
- Weight: Light, Regular, or Medium
- Use for: H3 onward, paragraphs, buttons, captions, footnotes
- Voice: clean, approachable, modern

### Hierarchy rule
- Pair the serif (Playfair Display) for emotional and headline moments with the sans-serif (Montserrat) for everything else.
- Never mix multiple serifs. Never use Playfair Display for body copy.

## Color palette

### Primary
- Soft black or deep charcoal (NOT pure #000000): suggests refinement over harshness
- Warm white or off-white: backgrounds, negative space
- Muted neutral tones: warm taupe, soft sage, dusty blue (use sparingly as accents)

### Accent
- A single signature accent color may be applied per campaign or asset, drawn from the warm neutral palette
- Avoid bright, saturated colors. Avoid pure black on pure white (too harsh for the brand).

### Rule
The Moxie palette is quiet. Color earns its place. Use it for emphasis, never for decoration.

## Layout principles

- Editorial spacing. Generous margins.
- Asymmetric balance over rigid symmetry where it adds intentionality.
- Photography-forward: real homes, real spaces, real people.
- Avoid stock photography that feels generic or "luxury template."
- High-quality lifestyle and architectural imagery only.

## Photography direction

- Natural light over heavy editing.
- Real homes. Real families. Real interiors.
- No staged "luxury fantasy" imagery (champagne flutes, marble close-ups with no context, infinity pools as the default).
- Honest framing that shows the home as livable.

## Listing presentation specs

### Print and digital flyer
- Playfair Display headline (property address or signature feature)
- Montserrat body for description
- Endless knot icon in header or footer
- High-resolution photography as the visual anchor
- Allison Gaddy contact info, Moxie Signature Group, eXp Realty (TREC required)

### Social media listing post
- Carousel format on Instagram
- First slide: lifestyle hero image with one Playfair Display line of copy
- Middle slides: rotation of best property photos with brief Montserrat captions
- Final slide: Moxie endless knot icon, address, "DM for private showing" or similar warm CTA

### Listing presentation (for sellers)
- Playfair Display section headers
- Montserrat body copy
- Endless knot watermark
- Premium photography
- eXp Realty included on legal/disclosure slides

## Patterns to avoid

- Em dashes anywhere on visual assets.
- Pure black backgrounds (use deep charcoal or soft black).
- Bright, saturated colors that read "real estate flyer."
- Keys, house silhouettes, generic real estate iconography.
- Stock luxury photography (champagne, infinity pools, gold details).
- Fonts outside Playfair Display + Montserrat.
- Heavy gradients or skeuomorphic effects.
- Clip art or cartoon-style illustrations.
- All-caps headlines for emphasis (use weight or scale instead).

## Brand-aligned vibe references

When directing photography or design, anchor the aesthetic in:
- Editorial home design magazines (Architectural Digest, Magnolia Journal, Kinfolk)
- Modern hospitality brands that feel premium and accessible
- Boutique architecture firms with Texas regional sensibility

Avoid the visual vocabulary of:
- Mass-market real estate brokerages
- High-end developer marketing that feels exclusionary
- Influencer-led "luxury lifestyle" aesthetics

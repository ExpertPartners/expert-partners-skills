# Moxie Voice and Content Reference

Read this for any longer-form Moxie content: listings, presentations, marketing campaigns, agent recruitment materials, blog posts, email sequences.

## Core voice principle

Moxie speaks like a trusted advisor who happens to have impeccable taste. Confident, informed, warm, never showy.

The reader should feel: respected, understood, and like they are being let in on something thoughtful, not sold to.

## Voice attributes (Moxie-specific calibration)

- Refined but not pretentious
- Warm but not casual
- Confident but not boastful
- Specific but not clinical
- Inviting but not pushy
- Premium but accessible

## How Moxie voice differs from Allison's personal voice

Allison's personal voice (used for FAM/EPIC content) is:
- Voice memo energy
- Short punchy sentences
- Direct, real-time, unpolished

Moxie voice is:
- Slower, more considered cadence
- Fuller sentences with rhythm and texture
- Polished but warm, never stiff
- Still no banned phrases, still no em dashes, still no fluff

When writing as Moxie, slow down. When writing as Allison personally, speed up.

## Banned phrases (carry over from Allison's voice rules)

- Amazing. Incredible. Mind-blowing.
- Just crush it. Go get em.
- Hustle harder. Grind mode.
- Great question. I love this.
- Any vague motivational fluff.

## Cliche phrases banned for Moxie specifically

- "Luxury living redefined"
- "Your dream home awaits"
- "Once in a lifetime"
- "Live your best life"
- "Welcome home"
- "Resort-style living"
- "Entertainer's dream"
- "Truly stunning" / "Truly remarkable"
- "Must see to appreciate"
- Anything that sounds like a 1990s glossy real estate ad

## Phrases that fit Moxie

- "Thoughtfully designed for"
- "Curated for the way you actually live"
- "Built around your priorities, not the market's"
- "Real homes for real families"
- "Premium service. No pretense."
- "Where elevated meets accessible"
- "The kind of home that earns its place"
- "Designed with intention"

## Listing copy framework

### Pattern: Lifestyle, story, details, place, invitation

#### 1. Lifestyle lead (1-2 sentences)
Open with what makes this home worth a second look. Frame it around how someone would actually live in it.

Example:
"This is the kind of Frisco home that earns its place. Designed for families who want premium finishes without losing the warmth of everyday living."

#### 2. Story walk (paragraph)
Walk through the home in story order, not feature-list order. Entry, primary living, kitchen, primary suite, secondary bedrooms, outdoor.

Example:
"Step in through a wide foyer that opens onto an open-plan living and dining space anchored by a stone fireplace. The kitchen sits at the heart, oversized island, custom cabinetry, and a walk-in pantry that actually fits a Costco run. The primary suite tucks away on the main floor with its own private patio access, while secondary bedrooms upstairs each pair with bath access designed for kids who are not sharing toothpaste with siblings."

#### 3. Elevated details (paragraph)
Name the finishes, fixtures, custom work, smart home, energy systems. Be specific. Avoid "high-end finishes" as a phrase. Name the actual things.

Example:
"Quartz counters throughout. Wide-plank European oak floors. Smart climate zoning across three levels. Tankless water heater. Tesla charger in the three-car garage. The kind of details you notice on day 100, not just day 1."

#### 4. Neighborhood anchor (paragraph)
Place the home in its community. Schools, walkability, amenities, drive times to what matters.

Example:
"Located in one of Frisco's most established neighborhoods, walkable to a top-rated elementary, ten minutes to the Star, twenty to downtown Plano. Trail access from the back gate. The kind of street where neighbors actually wave."

#### 5. Invitation close (1-2 sentences)
Direct, warm, low-pressure. No high-pressure CTAs.

Example:
"Schedule a private showing when you are ready. We will walk you through it without the rush."

## Email and campaign voice

### Subject lines
- Specific over clever
- Curious over hyped
- Personal over salesy

Examples that work:
- "A Frisco home you will probably love"
- "Three properties just hit the Prosper market"
- "Quick note about your home search"

Examples that do not:
- "🏠 AMAZING NEW LISTING! 🔥"
- "You won't believe this home"
- "Don't miss out!"

### Body copy
- Speak directly. Use "you." Use "we" for Moxie.
- Lead with value or insight, not the ask.
- One clear next step per email.
- Sign off warmly with name + Moxie + Allison's contact info.

## Agent recruitment voice (when writing for the Moxie team)

When the content targets potential Moxie team members (real estate agents who might join the team):

- Lead with the standard of work, not the perks.
- Speak to their professional identity: thoughtful, growth-oriented, premium-quality agents.
- Emphasize team philosophy: real homes for real families, premium service without pretense.
- Be honest about the bar. Moxie is not for every agent.

## Photography and visual copy alignment

When writing copy that pairs with imagery:
- Let the image carry the emotional weight.
- Copy supports, does not duplicate.
- Avoid "as you can see in the photo" language.
- Trust the reader.

## Self-check before delivering Moxie content

1. Em dashes? Scan and rewrite.
2. Cliche real estate phrases? Replace with specific language.
3. Banned phrases (from FAM voice rules)? Replace.
4. Does it sound like a trusted advisor or a salesperson? If salesperson, rewrite.
5. Specific finishes and details named, or vague "high-end" language? Replace vagueness with specifics.
6. eXp Realty included where TREC requires? Verify.
7. Endless knot icon (not keys) referenced for visuals? Verify.
8. eXp Luxury referenced anywhere? Remove.
9. Allison Gaddy spelled correctly? Verify.

If any check fails, fix before delivering.

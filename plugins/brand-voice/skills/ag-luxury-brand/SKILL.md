# AG Luxury Brand System (public brand: Allison Gaddy)

## Canon and live context

Canonical repo: `ExpertPartners/ag-luxury`. `docs/DECISION-LOG.md` is the append-only ruling record and wins over this skill wherever they conflict. `docs/governance/` holds the approved claim set. The estate-brain does not index ag-luxury (verified 2026-08-02), so this skill is the working reference for sessions without the repo.

Visual tokens (color, type, spacing) are owned by AG Luxury Brand Guide v1, ruled authoritative by Allison 2026-07-25 as D024 in the decision log. Do not restate or improvise visual tokens here or anywhere; read D024. Its one governing idea carries into copy: restraint is the luxury signal, and all warmth comes from photography, never from decoration.

## Naming ruling (never deviate)

- The public-facing brand is Allison Gaddy, the full name. "AG Luxury" never appears in consumer copy. AG Luxury is the internal workspace and registry name only, per aura ADR 0045.
- Rejected names, never use: Allison Gaddy Luxury, AG Luxury, Gaddy Luxury, Gaddy Homes, The Allison Gaddy Group, The Gaddy Group. The full name is the brand. Luxury is communicated by the work, not written into the name.
- The lockup reads Allison Gaddy, Luxury Real Estate Advisor, eXp Luxury. The exact title is verified against current TREC and eXp rules before any publication (the strategist brief flags this verification as a standing pre-publication step; the formal listing bio uses Broker Associate | eXp Luxury, her licensed title).
- The AG monogram is the secondary mark. Official eXp Luxury artwork stays a separate approved mark, never redrawn or fused into the Allison Gaddy identity.
- Spelling locks: eXp (lowercase e, uppercase XP). Allison Gaddy, two Ls. eXp Realty co-branding appears only where TREC requires it; eXp Luxury co-branding is deliberate and full for this brand.

## Positioning

- The frame: The Producer Came Home. A trained film producer (MFA, Peter Stark Producing Program, USC School of Cinematic Arts) who came home to Dallas to sell at the top of her home market under her own name.
- Core line, exact: "Your home, produced like a film. Priced like an asset. Handled like you are my only client."
- Four pillars, every piece of content sits under exactly one: produced not promoted; priced like an asset; documented not promised; Texas depth.
- One position only: the top of the North Dallas market and the western ranch corridor. Business below the luxury line refers out. The brand never stretches down-market.
- The counter-position is implied, never named: the contrast is with "brands that count impressions." Attention is easy, outcomes are hard. Receipts, not reach.

## Audience

Consumers only. Luxury sellers and buyers, estate, ranch, and equestrian owners, executives and relocations, in North Dallas (Frisco, Prosper, Celina, Plano, the Park Cities corridor) and the western ranch corridor (Parker County, Fort Worth side). Never agents. Agent attraction happens as a side effect of public work, never as this brand's voice or content lane. Organization numbers (900 agents, volume, rev share) never appear in consumer luxury copy; they belong to industry rooms and press only.

## Voice

- First person as Allison, or close third when the format requires it (formal bios, press boilerplate).
- Warm first-name storytelling. Editorial and exact. Full sentences, unhurried cadence, trusted-advisor register, not voice-memo casual and not corporate.
- (The receipts-over-sizzle mandate came OUT of this voice spec for now, ruled 2026-08-06, too firm, too much pressure. The rest of the ruled voice stands.)
- Producer framing throughout: a producer causes the thing to exist, finds the story, engineers the launch. Listings get a produced launch, not a promotion.
- Stillness and restraint. No hype, no urgency, no countdowns, no exclamation-heavy copy. Grace reads as confidence in this market.
- No coaching or recruiting energy ever. No black-and-gold luxury cliche, in copy or in look (D024 removed gold entirely). No cliches: never "Luxury living redefined," "Your dream home awaits," "Once in a lifetime."
- Discretion is a feature. The clientele buys it. No client names in marketing or stories without documented consent.

Language bank, reuse until strangers repeat them: The producer came home. Receipts, not reach. Produced like a film, priced like an asset. Attention is easy, outcomes are hard. Global reach, local proof. I do not chase seventeen billion strangers, I find the twelve buyers who matter. My brokerage serves the agent so I can serve you.

## Proof discipline

- Every public claim must survive both a fact check and a TREC read. A brand built on receipts dies of embarrassment the first time a claim wobbles.
- Exact numbers over rounded reach. Approximate denominators are flagged as approximate. Every statistic links to its source where one exists.
- The standing gate: every public asset (film, case study, press item, bio, page, post) gets a TREC compliance review before it ships. This is the gate, not a formality. No exceptions.
- Texas is a non-disclosure state. No sold price and no sold-to-list percentage appears publicly until the full compliance stack clears it (NTREIS rules, brokerage advertising policy, TREC truthful-advertising standards, written client consent). Safely publishable today: sold, sold on the first showing, days on market once verified against the closing statement. Full numbers are walked through in person, at the table, from the closing statements. Shoot the ritual, never the numbers.
- King Ranch (259 King Ranch Court): "sold on the first showing" is usable only per the MLS-status condition in canon. The claim rides the verified MLS record (MLS #21283114, microsite-verified per the consolidation report; closed July 2026). State it as fact about that sale, never as a pattern.
- Specific about the past, loud; silent about the future, always. Never a pattern claim ("my listings sell faster"). The language is this sale, this outcome.
- Honest verbs for the organization chapter: led, built the organization, poured into, coached, been inside. Never "I built 900 businesses." Never claim ICON status personally (21 ICON agents inside the organization is the true sentence).
- Superlative lock: "the top ranked film school in the country," attributed if pushed. Never an unqualified world's number one.
- Bella Cima (210 Bella Cima Drive, Austin) is framed as her final Austin sale before coming home to Dallas, never pushed as recent activity.
- Every competitor metric, if ever cited, is described as their self-reported marketing data.

## Banned

- All EP, EPIC, and FAM vocabulary: recruit, sponsor, revenue share, Break The Cycle, Think Big Build Smart, Free Agent anything, #eXpProud. This brand never pitches EPIC or FAM.
- Hustle language and hype. The "amazing" family (amazing, incredible, insane, epic-as-adjective). Exclamation-heavy copy.
- "AI" in seller-facing copy. Translate to precision, speed, and exposure. The AI story belongs to press and authority content only.
- Serhant or Ryan by name in any consumer-facing material. The contrast is always implied ("a celebrity real estate brand"). Industry keynote rooms are the only exception, per the playbook.
- Anything that frames the friend who joined the competitor negatively, anywhere, ever. Two producers, one door, two bets, mutual respect.
- Trash talk of any kind. Texas luxury punishes it.

## Formatting locks (inherited, never deviate)

- No em dashes anywhere. Use commas, periods, parentheses, or double hyphens.
- Colons and semicolons allowed in drafted prose, sparingly, never overused (ruled 2026-08-04). Commas, periods, parentheses, and double hyphens stay preferred.
- No bold or italics in copy (legal and MLS requirements are the only exception).

## Self-check before delivering anything

1. "AG Luxury" in consumer copy? Replace with Allison Gaddy.
2. Em dashes, colons, or semicolons in prose? Bold or italics? Rewrite.
3. Any sold price or percentage not compliance-cleared? Remove; route to the in-person walkthrough.
4. Any pattern claim or unattributed superlative? Rewrite as a specific past fact.
5. EP/EPIC/FAM vocabulary, coaching energy, or agent-facing framing? Remove.
6. Hype, urgency, exclamation marks, luxury cliches? Rewrite still and exact.
7. Organization numbers in consumer copy? Remove.
8. "AI" said to a seller? Translate to precision, speed, exposure.
9. Competitor named in consumer material? Make the contrast implied.
10. TREC compliance review scheduled before ship? It is the gate.

If any check fails, fix before delivering.

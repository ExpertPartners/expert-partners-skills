# Quick Update

One job. Tell Allison where things stand in three short lists she can read in under a minute, the way a first grader would say it.

Source: Allison's October 31, 2026 request for a very brief first grade CEO style update in the Google Workspace access strategy audit session. The HQ Rulebook wins wherever this differs.

## Trigger

The skill fires on these and nothing else.

1. A message that is only quick update, quick update please, simple update, short version, or the short version.
2. Give me a quick update, give me the short version, or quick update on this, said as the whole request.
3. A request for what is important, what is still outstanding, and what you recommend, in plain words or like I'm a first grader.

## What never fires it

The words quick or update inside a normal sentence. Send Don a quick update does not fire it. Update the listing does not fire it. Answer those the normal way.

CEO brief is Teach Me's own step opener, so it never fires this skill. Q and ask me clarifying questions belong to ask-me-questions. Sixth grade and kindergarten belong to kindergartner-mode, which gives one physical action at a time. Quick Update gives the whole picture at once and asks for no action unless one is truly blocking.

## What to do when it fires

1. Verify first. Read the live state of what this session worked on, such as git status, the Job Tracker row, logs, test output, or the file itself. Never report from memory what a check can answer. If something cannot be checked, say it is not checked.
2. Write the heading, Quick update, super simple.
3. Write the three lists below, in this order, each with its short heading on its own line.
4. Write the closing status line for the surface (see below).
5. Stop. No extra recap, no menu, no question unless a real decision is waiting on Allison.

## The three lists

What's important. Two to four bullets, the things she must know. Lead with what changed and what works, then what is protected. One fact per bullet. Say what you tested when you tested it.

What's still outstanding. Two to four bullets. Each one names the thing, who does it, and when it happens, with the date and time first when there is one. Say who owns it, you, a scheduled job, or her. End with Nothing is needed from you right now when that is true, and when it is not true, name the one thing she must do and where.

What I recommend. Two or three bullets. Give a recommendation, not a menu. Say what to keep, what to say yes to and why, and what to leave alone. If she must decide something, make that the first bullet and state the decision in one sentence.

## Reading level and shape

First grade words. Short sentences. One idea per sentence. No jargon unless the plain words come in the same breath, like the old keys, the ones we used before. Numbers and dates stay exact, never rounded to sound simpler. The whole brief stays under about 150 words.

Do not soften or hide a problem to keep it simple. If something failed or is unverified, it goes in the first list in plain words.

## Closing line

Claude Code and Codex. End with exactly one verified SESSION STATUS line, chosen by the standing tests in the Code close rule, Waiting on you, Still working, SAFE TO ARCHIVE, or NOT safe to archive. Verify it live in the same turn. If waits exist, say where they are lodged, such as the Job Tracker row number.

Cowork. Follow the Cowork close rule. Give an advance cue only when a next step exists. When nothing remains, say exactly We're done here. You can archive this session.

## What it never changes

The work and the rules. The doctrine, the verification duty, the approval gates, and the formatting locks stay at full strength. No em dashes. Colons and semicolons sparingly. No bold or italics. Links are clickable file names, never raw URLs. Only the length and the register change, the thinking does not shrink.

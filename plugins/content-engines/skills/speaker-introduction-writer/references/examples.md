# Speaker Introduction Writer, worked examples

All people below are fictional. The examples teach selection, not wording. Never reuse their sentences.

## 1. Weak versus strong

Weak, a resume read aloud.

> Our next speaker is the founder and CEO of ABC Consulting. She has more than 24 years of experience, has worked with 320 companies in 17 countries, is a three-time bestselling author, was named to the Top 50 Leadership Experts list, hosts a podcast, has been featured in Forbes and Entrepreneur, and lives in Austin with her husband and two children.

Strong, same person, less information, more meaning.

> Most leaders know how to grow a company. Far fewer know what to do when the company grows faster than they do. Sarah Mitchell has spent more than two decades inside that exact problem, helping founders navigate the point where personal leadership has to become organizational leadership. And a lot of what she believes about that transition came from making it badly herself before she learned to make it well. Today, she's going to take us inside that shift. Please welcome Sarah Mitchell.

The mental box matters. "Jane has worked in real estate for 27 years and closed 4,600 transactions" says experienced agent. "Jane has spent her career studying what happens when highly productive agents build businesses that eventually own them" says I need to hear this.

## 2. Same bio, two topics

Source. Marcus Reynolds, founder and CEO of Apex Growth Partners. 22 years, founded seven companies, raised more than $80 million in venture capital, more than $450 million cumulative revenue, more than 1,200 employees, featured in Forbes, Inc., Entrepreneur, and Fast Company, Entrepreneur of the Year from the Southwest Business Council, author of Scaling Without Chaos, host of The Founder Edge podcast.

Topic, the moment a founder has to stop being the center of the company. Almost everything is cut. The fact that matters is that he has crossed that transition more than once.

> There comes a point in building a company when the habits that made the founder successful become the very habits holding the company back. Marcus Reynolds knows that transition because he's lived it across multiple businesses, learning what changes when a founder has to stop being the answer to everything. Today, he's going to take us inside that shift and what it requires from the person at the center of it. Please welcome Marcus Reynolds.

Topic, raising institutional capital. Now the $80 million earns its place.

> Raising capital is one thing. Knowing which capital actually helps you build the company you want is another. Across multiple businesses, Marcus Reynolds has raised more than $80 million and lived with the decisions that come after the money arrives. Today, he's going to talk about capital from the founder's side of the table, including the questions that matter long after the pitch is over. Please welcome Marcus Reynolds.

There is no best bio. There is a best selection of facts for this moment.

## 3. Real estate, the number that flips

Source. Angela Torres, third-generation REALTOR®, 31 years in residential real estate, leads 42 agents across six offices, annual production over $780 million, personally closed more than 2,000 transactions, Diamond Chairman's Circle Award 11 times, serves on three industry boards, past president of her state association.

Topic, creating clients for life. The $780 million is impressive and explains nothing.

> Real estate gives us plenty of ways to measure a transaction. Angela Torres has spent three decades paying attention to what happens after one. As a third-generation REALTOR® and longtime industry leader, she has built her business around relationships that keep extending well beyond the closing table. Today, she's going to challenge us to think differently about where the real lifetime value of a client relationship begins. Please welcome Angela Torres.

Topic, running a multi-office sales organization. Now the scale is the authority.

> A sales organization feels very different when it grows beyond one office, one leader, and one way of doing things. Angela Torres leads 42 agents across six offices and a business producing more than $780 million annually, giving her a firsthand view of what has to become systematic as an organization scales. Today, she's going to take us inside the operating decisions that make that growth possible. Please welcome Angela Torres.

Same number, different relevance. That is why there is no blanket rule to remove numbers.

## 4. The credential that survives

Source. Dr. Maya Chen, PhD in organizational psychology from Stanford, 18 years advising executives, more than 120 Fortune 500 clients in 14 countries, more than 600 workshops, three books, appearances on CNBC, Bloomberg, and NPR. Topic, leading through uncertainty.

> Most leaders are comfortable making decisions when they have enough information. Leadership gets much more interesting when they don't. Organizational psychologist Maya Chen has spent her career studying and advising leaders inside exactly those conditions, when certainty disappears but the decision still has to be made. Today, she's going to show us what changes when leadership has to happen before the answer is obvious. Please welcome Dr. Maya Chen.

The doctorate stays because it is the authority. The 600 workshops do not. Her title is used in the handoff because it is her professional form of address.

## 5. Useless aggregate

Source. David Brooks, strategist, executive coach, author, podcast host, certified Scaling Up coach, former EOS implementer, Kellogg graduate, 16 years, coached 850 entrepreneurs whose companies generated more than $6 billion combined. Topic, why entrepreneurs avoid the hard conversation.

> Entrepreneurs are often willing to take enormous business risks while avoiding one uncomfortable conversation for six months. David Brooks has spent years coaching founders inside that contradiction, watching what happens when the conversation everyone is avoiding becomes the conversation the company most needs. Today, he's going to help us get honest about why we do it and what changes when we stop. Please welcome David Brooks.

## 6. The accuracy trap

Source. Rachel Morgan, internationally recognized keynote speaker, five-time bestselling author, former Olympic athlete, television commentator, entrepreneur, philanthropist, leadership expert, spoken in 38 countries, featured by CNN, ABC, NBC, CBS, Forbes, Fortune, and The Wall Street Journal, nonprofit has raised $12 million for youth sports. Topic, rebuilding after a major setback.

Fails the gate. Reads well, invents a story.

> Rachel Morgan knows something about the distance between the life you planned and the life you suddenly have. Her career began at the highest level of competitive sport, but some of the experiences that ultimately shaped her work on resilience came after the plans stopped working.

Nothing in the source says a setback happened. That second sentence is fiction.

Passes, built only on what the source supports.

> Every one of us has had a plan stop working. Rachel Morgan started her career as an Olympic athlete, in a world where the distance between the plan and the result can come down to a single moment. Today, she's going to talk about what rebuilding really asks of us when going back is no longer the option. Please welcome Rachel Morgan.

Then one line after it. Ask Rachel's team whether there is a setback story she shares publicly, because that would make this introduction much stronger.

# Idea Check

Most weak posts were weak ideas before anyone wrote a word. This check catches that in thirty seconds.

## The four criteria

**Specific (0-10)**
Is the idea narrow enough to deliver something usable in one post?
- 3/10: "Tips for better sleep"
- 8/10: "Why waking at 3am every night is a blood sugar problem, and the one snack that fixes it for most people"

**Fresh (0-10)**
Is this a take the reader hasn't already seen twenty times this month? Pull from the profile's "what I know that others miss." Fresh doesn't require being contrarian. It can be a familiar topic with a new mechanism, a new number, or a new moment.
- 3/10: "Why you need an email list"
- 8/10: "Your email list isn't dead. Your subject lines are. The 4-word test I run before every send."

**Her moment (0-10)**
Is the idea tied to a real moment in their life they'll recognize instantly?
Score this criterion only on whether the idea names or clearly points to a real, recognizable moment for this audience. Evidence that readers will recognize a moment is separate from permission to publish a quote, testimonial or result. Missing prevalence data, publication permission or profile fields, or doubt about whether one moment can carry a whole post, does not lower this score. Judge format fit under Fits the format, and apply permission limits to the wording of hooks and angles.
- 3/10: "Improving team communication"
- 8/10: "The Slack message you send at 9pm that makes your team dread Monday"

**Fits the format (0-10)**
Can the full payoff land in one post in the intended format? Big topics crammed into one caption score low. Suggest a series or a different format when that's the fix.

## Verdicts

- **WRITE IT:** all four are 7 or higher.
- **SHARPEN IT:** any criterion is 5 or 6, nothing lower.
- **SKIP IT:** any criterion is 4 or below. Say what can't be saved in one line.

## Three sharper angles

Always give three, even when the verdict is WRITE IT. Each angle gets:

```
Angle 1: [short name]
Hook: "[a first line written for this reader]"
The takeaway: [the one thing they'll know or be able to do after]
Best format: [carousel / reel / text post / thread / story sequence] · [why, in a few words]
```

Make the three angles different from each other. A good spread: one teaches a mechanism, one names a mistake, one tells a story that mirrors the reader. When no real story is supplied or permitted, the mirror angle may be a clearly labeled hypothetical scenario. It must not present itself as a real customer event, quote unpermitted customer language, or add invented proof, numbers or causal rules. Never invent stats or client results inside an angle. Use placeholders if needed.

Check each hook and takeaway against the supplied source boundaries, not just its score. Preserve the distinction between a stated fact and a proposed angle: do not infer an order from an unordered list or a universal cause from an audience problem. Mark new frames as suggestions and hypothetical situations as hypothetical, not approved company teaching or real customer stories. Use the audience's stated pronouns or neutral reader references, regardless of the fixed `Her moment` rubric label. Do not invent proof, permission, price, timing, urgency or a sales CTA.

Read every hook and takeaway alone, without its angle title, suggestion label or external disclaimer. Its literal assertions must still be supported at their stated scope. A proposed exercise can invite the reader to inspect a problem; it cannot declare an unverified cause or guarantee the exercise fixes it. An open question cannot presume an unverified diagnosis. Revise the sentence itself when it overclaims. Keep specific supported language and the three-angle format. Apply neutral reader prose to the critique and scoring reasons too.

End with: "Draft whichever one pulls you and paste it back. I'll score it."

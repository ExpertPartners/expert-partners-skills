# Participant Results Brief

Turn one real participant's evidence into one concise internal page. This is not a testimonial, a cohort report or a public success story.

## Read the evidence first

Use the supplied request, participant record, artifact, measurement record and permission record. Read the owning business's current voice and save-place instructions when available. Do not ask again for facts already established by those sources.

Separate source facts from interpretation. Keep each fact's source link or file name and date beside the field it supports. An unread source stays unread. A test fixture stays labeled as a test and never becomes a participant record.

## Handle gaps and conflicts

If no real participant record exists, do not create a fictional participant or a completed brief. Ask once for the missing evidence in one batch.

> Send the participant's actual starting point and its source, the artifact or workflow they built and its current stage, the metric with its unit, baseline, comparison period and source, and any documented permission with its exact scope and date. Unknown items can stay unknown.

If some evidence exists, retain the supported facts and mark missing fields unknown, unmeasured or not supplied. Label the page incomplete. Do not turn a missing measurement into a zero or an estimated improvement. Ask only for the missing evidence, in the same single batch.

If sources conflict, do not finalize the brief or silently choose the later, larger or more convenient claim. Identify the conflicting field and both sources, preserve uncontested facts, and ask for one authoritative correction. A revised source changes only the fields it supports. Keep the earlier source and the reason for the change traceable.

## Write the four fields

Use these headings in this order, with a short internal or incomplete status above them when needed. Do not add a cohort rollup.

### Starting point

State the participant's actual starting situation. Include the source and date. Do not invent an identity, problem or baseline to make the narrative work.

### What they built

Name the real artifact or workflow and link it when available. State its verified stage as an idea, draft, test, launch or recurring use. A demonstration is not a launch, and a launch alone does not prove recurring use.

### Measured result

Name the metric, unit, baseline, comparison period and measurement source. Distinguish measured results from the participant's self-report. Use only the precision supported by the source. If the baseline, period or method is missing, say what is missing rather than implying a valid before-and-after comparison.

Keep one participant's evidence separate from a cohort average. Do not claim that the program caused the change, or that one participant proves cohort performance. Omit unverified aggregates. Do not add cohort price, size, duration or dates.

### Permission to share

State whether permission is documented, unknown or refused. Include its source, date and exact allowed scope, such as name, artifact, result, quote and audience. Public availability is not permission. Unknown permission stays unknown.

Keep the brief internal by default. When permission is limited to an anonymized result, exclude the participant's name, identifying artifact details, quotes and other unapproved details from any shareable version. Do not publish, send, contact the participant or broaden permission as part of this skill.

## Save and check

Save in the owning business's existing results or evidence home when its instructions identify one. Preserve an existing brief's location on revision. If the destination is unknown, return the internal draft and state that it is not saved rather than inventing a home. Follow the active document-formatting rules and deliver a named link only after a save succeeds.

Before delivery, check that every factual claim has a source, every unknown remains explicit, build stages stay distinct, measurement limits are visible and sharing stays within documented scope. Report the actual save and invocation state. A source read or manual replay is not proof of a native marketplace invocation or a completed participant run.

## 0A. Proactive terminality -- zero freedom (v1.11)

Ruled by Allison 2026-09-01, ruling of record aura intake/2026-09-01-ruling-table-and-worker-lifecycle-terminality.md. The failure this kills, in her words: a mission table can complete its original mission, but unless she explicitly asks for a wrap or notices the work is over, the chair stays conversationally available and later starts absorbing adjacent work. **Allison must never be the person who notices that a table should already be closed.**

**The trigger is acceptance evidence, not a request.** A mission table runs the terminality check the moment any of these lands, in the same turn, unprompted:

- a worker carry-back is accepted, meaning its report has been read and its claims taken as good;
- a required PR, merge, deploy, or verification the mission named finishes;
- the last named dependency in the table's waiting-on state closes;
- any other evidence arrives that satisfies, or plainly cannot satisfy, the table's `DONE WHEN`.

**Who is exempt, and it is never a soft call.** The check runs only for a mission table, one carrying the `MISSION` and `DONE WHEN` contract from deliberate Table Birth (lane-spawn v1.7). A Scratch or general-purpose conversation and a standing-subject table under decision 0066 carry no contract, are never evaluated, and are **never nagged to archive merely because a topic ended**. Reaching the end of a subject is not the end of a table that never had a mission. Silence is the correct behavior there, and a wrap offered to a Scratch conversation on its own initiative is the failure, exactly as much as a mission table that never notices it is finished.

**The check itself, three steps.** Read the table's own `MISSION` and `DONE WHEN` from its durable record, never from a recollection of what the work felt like. Compare the landed evidence against that `DONE WHEN`, and against nothing else. Then take exactly one of two branches.

**Branch one, the mission is achieved.** Run the full wrap, steps 1 through 5, including the Table Closeout Gate at 4A, which is what actually earns the verdict. The check is the trigger, never a shortcut past the gate, and an unclear gate obligation blocks Mission achieved here exactly as it does when Allison asks for the wrap herself. Then say so plainly, in one sentence she does not have to interpret, on the pattern: `Mission achieved. Unless you want to give this table a genuinely new mission, you can archive it.` Do not invent successor work merely because interesting findings exist (section 4B). Do not hedge, do not soften it into a question, and do not ask whether she would like a wrap; the wrap already ran.

**Branch two, the mission is not achieved.** Say that plainly and **name the one real blocking acceptance or dependency item**, the specific thing whose closing would flip the verdict, with its named condition and its stale date lodged in the lane ledger's waiting-on column. One named thing, never a list; when several are open, name the one that unblocks the rest. A vague `idle until something resumes` posture is banned outright, as is every equivalent of "we can pick this up later" (operating-doctrine, Decide Now). A wait with no named condition is not a wait, it is a ripe question hiding.

**What this never becomes.** No daemon, no sweeper, no watcher, no nudge machinery, no board card, no second registry, and no separate closeout system. The check is a behavior this skill performs at a moment that already happens, and the durable state it reads and writes is the one that already exists.

## 3A. Final uncaptured-memory sweep -- zero freedom (v1.12)

Ruled into this protocol 2026-09-04 by the Immediate Pain Controls campaign (issues #15 and #16). Allison is not the Estate's detection mechanism for what deserves to survive a session, and the wrap is the last moment anything can be caught before the transcript stops being read.

Run the `estate-memory-router` gate once over the closing session, after the canonical pushes in step 3 and before flags ship in step 4, so anything the sweep captures is already durable when the verdict is written.

What the sweep asks, exactly once, over the whole session: is there anything here a cold future worker would need, or whose loss would cause drift, rework, contradiction, or lost provenance, that has NOT been given a durable home by the record, the continuity note, the ledger row, or a staged kickoff?

Route each qualifying item to its existing destination under the router's six routes. A future obligation goes to `remember-this` and gets one open issue in the owning repo carrying a mechanical `RETURN WHEN` line. Evidence and working knowledge go to the owning repo's intake. A founder decision is preserved as a candidate and is never promoted; only Allison's exact `Lock that` does that, under decisions 0060 and 0061. A canon or rule change fires `canon-sync-protocol`, which step 5's promotion gate already requires.

Bounds, all load-bearing:

- **The record and the continuity note already capture most of it.** The sweep looks for what they did NOT capture, and it never duplicates them. An item already durably held is not swept again.
- **Nothing qualifying is the normal result, and it is reported in silence.** A wrap does not announce that its sweep found nothing.
- **No secrets, ever**, to any destination. Route the 1Password reference, never the value.
- **A failed or unverified sweep write is stated plainly**, exactly like a failed intake push in step 3, and never reported as captured. A false receipt ends Allison's own carrying of the item, which is worse than no capture.
- **Capture is not activation.** The sweep never starts the work, never spawns a lane on its own, and never re-scopes the closing mission. Adjacent work still routes under section 4B.

A wrap that skips this sweep is a partial wrap, the failure condition.

## 4. Flags ship as staged kickoffs -- zero freedom on the mechanism

Ruled by Allison 2026-08-07 in the Follow-Through Engine lane. The mission is unchanged, no flag ever dies in a document. The mechanism is this and only this.

Every flag in the session record's Open questions and flags section leaves the wrap as a staged kickoff block, a ready-to-paste continuity prompt that starts a new conversation to address that flag. This is the lane-spawn pattern applied at wrap time, so each block follows PASTE 1 in the lane-spawn skill: envelope lines first, the lane's one job, why it exists, the standing state it inherits, its do-not-absorb list, its first move, and its return path. Outside each block, two labels, plainly, the destination (which Claude project, which surface) and the exact name for the new conversation.

Allison pulls the staged kickoffs at her own tempo. Five flags opening five conversations is fine and manageable; she ruled it. The open conversation IS the tracking. No reminder machinery, no nudge, no watcher, no due date, no card. None of it is built and none of it is wanted, because the lockstep march replaces it.

No flag ever gets a board card. Board cards are reserved for genuinely far-horizon items only. The Founder Active Work board holds missions and far-horizon markers, never working state. A project that looks complete on a board while ten unnoticed things sit on it is the exact failure this rule kills.

Before a flag is staged, classify it. Every flag is one of exactly two things, and there is no third.

- Ripe. Its inputs are complete, so the question comes to Allison immediately, in order, in the live conversation, and gets decided there. A ripe question is never staged and never deferred; see the Decide Now clause in the operating-doctrine. Staging a ripe question is the failure, not the safe choice.
- Waiting. It waits on a named condition, a third party, elapsed time, Allison's hands, or a running process. It goes in the lane ledger's waiting-on column with the condition and the date it goes stale, per the No Later Commandment. A wait with no named condition is not a wait, it is a ripe question hiding.

Sequencing note so this does not fight the one-block-per-response rule in code-block-routing. A wrap's staged kickoff set is the named exception to that rule and the only one. Those blocks are a menu Allison draws from at her own tempo, not a sequence of dependent steps, which is what the one-block rule protects her from. Everything else in every other response stays at one block.

## 4A. The Table Closeout Gate (decision 0075) -- zero freedom

Ruled by Allison 2026-08-26, ruling of record aura decisions/0075-table-closeout-gate-and-worker-only-archive-vocabulary.md, delivered here at v1.10. Before any table may reach Mission achieved, the wrap runs six obligations. Each one clears with evidence or blocks the verdict. Table type never reduces obligations one, two, three, five, or six, which apply to every table wrap regardless of type.

One. Every actual Allison-issued Lock That signal completed the decision 0060 flow through its delivered receipt. The obligation is Allison's real lock commands, never lexical appearances of the phrase. Quoted source material, pasted prompts or instructions containing the phrase, examples, AI-authored text, and discussion of what Lock That means are all excluded. This check verifies completion only and never re-litigates or reinterprets a lock.

Two. Every approved Book Moment is captured to the book repo with its required frontmatter and its USCO contribution log entry. Approved means Allison identified something as a Book Moment or directly instructed its capture, or the AI surfaced a candidate and Allison explicitly accepted it. A noticed-but-unapproved candidate may be offered once at wrap and Allison's no clears it. Book Moment capture is narrative preservation, never governance, and it never substitutes for Lock That in either direction.

Three. No table closes over a worker it still owns. Every Claude Code worker the table dispatched must have completed decision 0070's closure loop and reached its proper worker disposition, which since v1.11 includes its terminal transition: the worker released every monitor, check-in, subscription, scheduled wakeup, and background agent it owned (session-steward v1.3, section 7A), and its ledger row states its true current state rather than a stale wait. A worker that is genuinely finished but still advertising itself as active in the ledger is an unclear obligation three, cleared by reconciling the row here, not by asserting the worker is fine. A worker genuinely waiting on a named condition keeps its owning table at Mission not achieved for as long as the table owns it. Worker ownership is never silently transferred to a successor table to permit closure. Cloud-worker registration in the lane ledger row remains mandatory and a missing registration is repaired at wrap, but registration alone never makes an open worker compatible with table closure.

Four. Every durable artifact the table is actually required to maintain under current doctrine is verified and consistent with live state. For a lane-backed work table that means the session record, the continuity note, and a current lane ledger row. For a legitimate non-lane-backed table under decision 0066, standing subject, planning, or personal reflection, that means only the artifacts that table genuinely owns, and no lane row, work-state row, or continuity note is invented solely to satisfy the gate. Raw personal or reflective material is never pushed into work-state machinery or a body-of-work repo because a table is closing. An already-written continuity note found to conflict with live state is superseded by a corrected note, never unwritten, because intake is append-only history.

Five. Every evidenced cross-surface obligation is executed, dispatched, or shipped to its exact destination table. An obligation exists only on evidence of a real dependency, an explicit promised return, accepted dispatch return criteria, a named dependency in live lane state, a decision or input another existing table is explicitly waiting for, or continuing work that must be handed to a named successor. Absent such evidence, zero clears. Another surface finding the information interesting is not an obligation.

Six. Continuing work names its successor table exactly, under deliberate Table Birth. If the continuing work is the same table's own mission, that is not succession, that is Mission not achieved.

Surface-specific and product-specific mechanics never live in this skill. They live in dated wiring and evidence records.

## 4B. A completed table never invents a sequel -- zero freedom (v1.11)

Ruled by Allison 2026-09-01, ruling of record aura intake/2026-09-01-ruling-table-and-worker-lifecycle-terminality.md. Once a table's original mission is achieved, adjacent or new work does **not** silently extend it. **A completed historical chair never decides its own future mission**, however interesting the thing it just noticed.

Where the new work goes, in this order. To the existing table that already owns that subject, named exactly, if one exists. Otherwise it is deliberately born as its own table under Table Birth (lane-spawn v1.7), carrying its own `MISSION` and `DONE WHEN`, and it ships as a staged kickoff under step 4 like every other flag. Genuinely trivial in-lane cleanup that is a natural extension of the finished work is finished in place under decision 0083 rather than staged, and it never reopens the mission.

What is banned, plainly. Rewriting or widening a `DONE WHEN` so a finished table stays alive. Treating a discovered finding as proof the mission was never complete. Carrying a new job under an old table's name because the chair is already open and convenient. Allison alone gives a table a genuinely new mission, and she does it explicitly; a chair never grants one to itself.

The one honest exception. If the landed evidence shows the original mission was in fact **not** achieved, that is Mission not achieved under section 0A branch two, not a sequel. Say which of the two it is; the difference is whether the work serves the original `DONE WHEN` or a new one.
